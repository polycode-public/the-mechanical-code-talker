You are grading how faithfully a deterministic fact-extractor restated a source
document. You are NOT judging writing style, only meaning.

You are given two texts:

INPUT — the original source document.
RESTATEMENT — the extractor's output: the input's meaning re-expressed as a set
of plain canonical statements. It may be shorter than the input, and "(nothing
extracted)" means the extractor produced no statements at all.

Score meaning-preservation in BOTH directions, 0-2 each:

forward — does every claim in the RESTATEMENT follow from the INPUT?
  2 = every restated statement is supported by the input.
  1 = mostly supported, with a minor overreach.
  0 = the restatement asserts something the input does not say (an invented or
      confused fact). This is the fault that matters most: a wrong fact is worse
      than a missing one.

backward — does every claim in the INPUT appear in the RESTATEMENT?
  2 = the restatement captures essentially all of the input's factual content.
  1 = it captures some of the input's facts but drops others.
  0 = it captures little or none of the input's meaning.

Rules:
- Judge facts, not phrasing. "a dog is a kind of animal" and "dogs are animals"
  are the same claim.
- Missing facts lower `backward`, never `forward`. An honest partial restatement
  (nothing wrong, some facts dropped) should score forward 2, backward 1.
- "(nothing extracted)" is forward 2 (it asserts nothing wrong) and backward 0
  (it captured nothing), unless the input itself states no facts.
- If a direction cannot be judged, score it null. Do not score a direction the
  case did not ask about.
