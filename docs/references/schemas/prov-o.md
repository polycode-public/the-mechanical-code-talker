## Why tmct reads this

Every fact tmct stores records where it came from and when. PROV-O is the published vocabulary for
that. `src/adapters/memory/core.mjs` has carried the note "ext ref prov:wasDerivedFrom
(UNVERIFIED-pending-web-check)" on `mgx:derivedFrom` since it was written. This entry closes the
check. The answer is in `archive/PLAN_NORMATIVE.md` §4.1, and it is not the term the note guessed.

## The three starting-point classes

| term | definition (verbatim) |
|---|---|
| `prov:Entity` | "An entity is a physical, digital, conceptual, or other kind of thing with some fixed aspects; entities may be real or imaginary." |
| `prov:Activity` | "An activity is something that occurs over a period of time and acts upon or with entities; it may include consuming, processing, transforming, modifying, relocating, using, or generating entities." |
| `prov:Agent` | "An agent is something that bears some form of responsibility for an activity taking place, for the existence of an entity, or for another agent's activity." |

Declared subclasses: `prov:Entity` → `prov:Collection`, `prov:Plan`, `prov:Bundle`.
`prov:Agent` → `prov:Organization`, `prov:Person`, `prov:SoftwareAgent`.

The three are the model's spine. **They are disjoint in intent**, and that matters for tmct: a
vocabulary that lets one class stand for a person, a document and a process has flattened a
distinction PROV keeps.

## Starting-point properties

All are sub-properties of `prov:wasInfluencedBy`, except the two datatype properties.

| term | domain | range | definition (verbatim, abridged) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `prov:wasGeneratedBy` | Entity | Activity | "Generation is the completion of production of a new entity by an activity. This entity did not exist before generation and becomes available for usage after this generation." |
| `prov:wasDerivedFrom` | Entity | Entity | "A derivation is a transformation of an entity into another, an update of an entity resulting in a new one, or the construction of a new entity based on a pre-existing entity." |
| `prov:wasAttributedTo` | Entity | Agent | "Attribution is the ascribing of an entity to an agent." |
| `prov:wasAssociatedWith` | Activity | Agent | "An activity association is an assignment of responsibility to an agent for an activity, indicating that the agent had a role in the activity." |
| `prov:used` | Activity | Entity | "Usage is the beginning of utilizing an entity by an activity." |
| `prov:wasInformedBy` | Activity | Activity | "Communication is the exchange of an entity by two activities, one activity using the entity generated by the other." |
| `prov:actedOnBehalfOf` | Agent | Agent | "Delegation is the assignment of authority and responsibility to an agent … to carry out a specific activity as a delegate or representative." |
| `prov:startedAtTime` | Activity | `xsd:dateTime` | "The time at which an activity started." |
| `prov:endedAtTime` | Activity | `xsd:dateTime` | "The time at which an activity ended." |

`prov:wasInfluencedBy` is the umbrella over all of them:

> "Influence is the capacity of an entity, activity, or agent to have an effect on the character,
> development, or behavior of another by means of usage, start, end, generation, invalidation,
> communication, derivation, attribution, association, or delegation."

**That umbrella is the term tmct's `mgx:derivedFrom` actually means.** See `archive/PLAN_NORMATIVE.md` §4.1.

## Expanded terms tmct's model touches

| term | kind | domain | range | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `prov:hadPrimarySource` | object | Entity | Entity | sub-property of `wasDerivedFrom`. "A primary source … refers to something produced by some agent with direct experience and knowledge about the topic, at the time of the topic's study, without benefit from hindsight." |
| `prov:wasRevisionOf` | object | Entity | Entity | sub-property of `wasDerivedFrom`. "A revision is a derivation for which the resulting entity is a revised version of some original. The implication here is that the resulting entity contains substantial content from the original." |
| `prov:wasQuotedFrom` | object | Entity | Entity | sub-property of `wasDerivedFrom`. "A quotation is the repeat of (some or all of) an entity, such as text or image, by someone who may or may not be its original author." |
| `prov:generatedAtTime` | datatype | Entity | `xsd:dateTime` | "The time at which an entity was completely created and is available for use." |
| `prov:invalidatedAtTime` | datatype | Entity | `xsd:dateTime` | "The time at which an entity was invalidated (i.e., no longer usable)." |
| `prov:SoftwareAgent` | class | — | — | subclass of `prov:Agent`. "A software agent is running software." |
| `prov:Plan` | class | — | — | subclass of `prov:Entity`. "A plan is an entity that represents a set of actions or steps intended by one or more agents to achieve some goals." |
| `prov:Bundle` | class | — | — | subclass of `prov:Entity`. "A bundle is a named set of provenance descriptions, and is itself an Entity, so allowing provenance of provenance to be expressed." |

The spec's own guidance on picking among the derivation family:

> "The more specific subproperties of prov:wasDerivedFrom (i.e., prov:wasQuotedFrom,
> prov:wasRevisionOf, prov:hadPrimarySource) should be used when applicable."

## PROV has no mutation, and no trust

Two absences shape what tmct can borrow.

**Entities do not change.** PROV models a change as a *new* entity standing in `prov:wasRevisionOf`
to the old one. There is no "this entity was edited in place" term, so there is nothing for tmct's
`mgx:updatedAt` to map onto.

**PROV carries no trust or reliability model.** It records who said a thing, not whether to believe
them. `mgx:trustScore`, `mgx:trustInputs` and `mgx:sourceReliability` have no PROV referent.
Candidate literatures for that layer: Golbeck's work on trust propagation in social/semantic
networks; Artz & Gil, "A survey of trust in computer science and the Semantic Web" (*Journal of Web
Semantics* 5(2), 2007); the W3C Provenance Incubator Group's trust use cases. No W3C
Recommendation covers it, so tmct's terms stay `mgx:` and say why.

## The Source split — one flat class over three disjoint top classes

`tmct:Source` carries an `mgx:sourceType` whose values fall into all three PROV top classes:
`operator`, `teach` and `provider` are actors (`prov:Agent`); `corpus`, `corpusWeak`, `web` and
`extracted` are documents (`prov:Entity`); `entailed` is the trace of a rule firing
(`prov:Activity`). Those three classes are pairwise disjoint, so no single `prov:` parent is true of
`tmct:Source` as a whole.

The ontology recovers the split with three read-side subclasses — `tmct:AgentSource`,
`tmct:DocumentSource`, `tmct:ActivitySource` — each defined (`owl:equivalentClass`) as `tmct:Source`
restricted to its `mgx:sourceType` values and asserted under one PROV top class. This is derivation,
not migration: the classifying value is already stored on every Source. `core.mjs`'s
`provSourceClassFor(sourceType)` is the code-side reader.

The split is what lets attribution align properly. `mgx:statedBy` keeps `rdfs:seeAlso
prov:wasAttributedTo` at the property level — its range is all three kinds, so `subPropertyOf` would
over-claim the Agent range for documents and activities. The real alignment lives at the subclass
level: a `statedBy` edge whose object is a `tmct:AgentSource` lands on a `prov:Agent` and IS
`prov:wasAttributedTo`; to a `tmct:DocumentSource` it is nearer `prov:hadPrimarySource`, and to a
`tmct:ActivitySource`, `prov:wasGeneratedBy`.

## The qualification pattern

An unqualified binary relation can be restated through an intermediate Influence instance, which
then carries extra description. Given `e1 prov:wasDerivedFrom e2`:

```turtle
:bar_chart a prov:Entity ;
   prov:wasDerivedFrom :aggregatedByRegions ;
   prov:qualifiedDerivation [ a prov:Derivation ;
      prov:entity :aggregatedByRegions ;
      prov:hadGeneration :chart_plotting ;
      prov:hadActivity :chart_creation ] .
```

The class chain is `prov:Derivation` ⊑ `prov:EntityInfluence` ⊑ `prov:Influence`. Consumers should
recognise both forms and read the qualified one as implying the unqualified one.

tmct does not use this pattern and does not need to: `mgx:trustInputs` already carries the
"why this score" detail as a JSON literal. The pattern is recorded here because it is the
standard's answer to "the binary edge is not enough", which is the question tmct will hit if
per-edge provenance detail is ever wanted.

## Two things that will trip an implementation

- **`prov:atLocation` has a union domain** (`Activity or Agent or Entity or InstantaneousEvent`).
  The spec says the multiple domains exist "to suit multiple OWL Profiles". A naive domain-inference
  pass over it misfires.
- **`prov:Derivation` is a subclass of `prov:EntityInfluence`, not of `prov:Influence` directly.**
