Search backed knowledge base

One graph, grown three ways. Research a term over Simple English Wikipedia, teach a fact by typing it, or paste a document. Then ask a question with only the sources you trust switched on.

What it demonstrates

The other pages each show one way of getting facts in. This one puts all three side by side and then does something none of them can do alone: it lets you turn a source off and ask the same question again.

Three panels sit in a row. Research a term, which reads Simple English Wikipedia and grounds what it finds. Teach by telling, an ordinary sentence. Ingest documents, the same recognizer the ingest page uses.

Under them, an ask box with a checkbox per source. Uncheck a source and its facts stop answering. A question that only the unchecked source could answer becomes an honest miss, in front of you, on demand.

That is the demonstration worth doing live. Ask something, get an answer, untick where it came from, ask again, and watch it decline.

Chat you can replay

These come from the browser test that drives the page against a fixture Wikipedia, so the research run is reproducible.

Teach one fact and ingest a document. Two sources, two terms.

tmct> a wozzle is a kind of dog
source "taught" appears; fact count +1; recent list shows subject "wozzle"
ingest: A florp is a kind of animal. A florp has a tail. How are you today?
3 sentences read, 2 grounded, 1 skipped
source "ingest" appears; fact count +2

Asserted in test-e2e/pages-research.test.mjs.

Now scope it. The same question answers with every source on, and misses with only the ingest source on.

tmct> what is a wozzle
with every source checked: answers, naming wozzle
tmct> what is a wozzle
scoped to the ingest source only: a miss, it abstains
tmct> what is a florp
still scoped to ingest only: answers, naming florp

Asserted in test-e2e/pages-research.test.mjs, which checks the miss flag on each of the three calls. The taught fact goes out of scope; the ingested fact stays in.

A research run stops on its budget and says so, rather than quietly running out of links.

tmct> research owl
max nodes 3, max depth 2
research "owl" complete — 3 topics grounded (depth 2, budget 3) — node budget reached

Asserted in test-e2e/pages-research.test.mjs against a fixture wiki where Owl links to Bird, Night and Feather.

Reading a grown term back as prose, and asking for one that was never grown.

tmct> digest florp
a digest card whose paragraphs name florp and animal, over a (sources: ...) line
tmct> digest zzzznonexistentzzz
no card at all, and the miss text says nothing is stored

Asserted in test-e2e/pages-research.test.mjs, which checks the digest paragraphs, the sources line and the absence of a card for the unknown term.

What it works out

How a source is decided. Every fact's provenance tag folds to one page-level source key. Anything you typed becomes taught. Anything the ingest session grounded becomes ingest. Anything a Wikipedia read produced becomes research. Corpus rows become seed:<band>, one key per band.

How scoping works. Unchecking a source does not filter the answer. It removes those facts from a copy of the store and asks the real question against what is left. So a scoped answer is a genuine answer over a smaller graph, and a scoped miss is a genuine miss.

Entailed facts are kept under every scope. They rest on premises rather than on one source, so removing a source removes the premise and the entailment falls with it.

What a research run does. It fetches an article, grounds its summary, and follows links out to a bounded number of further topics. Three numbers bound it: how many links to follow from one article, how deep to go, and how many topics in total. The defaults are 5, 1 and 12; the caps are 12, 3 and 50. A run reports which bound stopped it.

What it stores. A researched fact is tagged research:<topic>@<depth> and cites the article by title, revision id and licence:

  • polar bear — <summary> (source: research article "Polar bear", Simple English Wikipedia, CC BY-SA 4.0 — <url>?oldid=912)

A live read scores 0.5. The curated reference pack scores 0.6. Something you taught scores 0.95. So a Wikipedia sentence never outranks you.

How it is built

src/services/research-viz.mjs renders the page. src/surfaces/web/research-browser-entry.mjs is the bundle, and it grows one in-memory store through three real engine paths rather than three copies of one.

The ingest panel imports groundTextToFacts directly from the ingest page's own entry module. It is the same function, not a second implementation.

The research lane is src/services/research.mjs. Its request grammar takes research <topic>, research next, research status and research stop, with optional limit N and depth D.

The curated reference pack is src/adapters/corpus/reference-pack.mjs: a gzipped term index plus gzipped JSONL shards, one shard read per hit. It never throws. A missing or corrupt pack is an ordinary miss. The browser swaps the filesystem loader for a fetch-backed provider over public/reference-pack/ through the same seam.

One snapshot function computes the source list, the recent facts and the hub terms together, so the three panels can never disagree with each other.

Related work

Retrieval that names its sources, and a question that can be asked over a subset of them.

  • Reiter, “On Closed World Data Bases”, in Logic and Data Bases, Plenum, 1978.Unchecking a source shrinks what is known. Under the open world assumption that produces a miss, not a denial.
  • Chow, “On optimum recognition error and reject tradeoff”, IEEE Trans. Information Theory 16(1), 1970.The scoped miss is the reject option, made switchable.
  • Green, Karvounarakis and Tannen, “Provenance Semirings”, PODS 2007.The algebra for which answers survive when a source is removed.
  • W3C, PROV-O, Recommendation, 30 April 2013.The provenance vocabulary the source keys fold from.
  • W3C, SKOS Reference, Recommendation, 18 August 2009.The concept scheme vocabulary for terms grown out of an encyclopaedia.
  • Artz and Gil, “A survey of trust in computer science and the Semantic Web”, Journal of Web Semantics 5(2), 2007.Read as candidate literature for source trust. The priors here are engineering, not a claim to implement this survey.
