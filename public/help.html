Asking, teaching, sharing, and what to do if something goes wrong

This page explains chat.html and world sharing. It doesn't run anything itself, and it doesn't hold a connection open.

What this is

tmct is a chatbot with no AI model in it anywhere. It answers only from facts it has been given. When it can't find an answer, it says so, instead of guessing. That refusal is the product working, not a gap in it.

Everything on these pages runs in your own browser. Nothing you type leaves your machine unless you choose to share it.

Asking and teaching

chat.html answers questions from the facts it already holds, and learns new ones when you teach it.

Ask a question. Type it in plain English. A grounded answer arrives with a citation naming where the fact came from.

what is a dog
dog is a kind of animal (source: corpus:human /r/IsA)
dog has tail (source: corpus:human /r/HasA)
dog can bark (source: corpus:human /r/CapableOf)

A miss. If nothing in its memory answers your question, it says so and tells you how to fix it. It never guesses.

what is a wug
I don't know "wug" yet — teach me directly, e.g. "wug is a <thing>" or "remember wug is a <thing>".

Teach a fact. Write a plain sentence like “X is a Y”. It repeats back what it stored. Ask about it again, and it cites you as the source.

cat is a pet
noted — remembered 1 fact: cat rdfs:subClassOf pet (every cat is a pet)
what is a cat
you told me: cat is a kind of pet (source: ace:chat:...@...)

Taught facts are kept on this device, best effort, and are never sent anywhere unless you share them yourself (see Sharing a world).

Sharing a world

Sharing connects two browsers directly, over WebRTC. There is no account and no server in between. Each side does one paste.

Inviting. Click the “invite” button. It mints a link, copies it to your clipboard, and opens the sharing panel. The status reads “waiting for their reply.” Send the link however you already talk to that person, and keep the tab open. When their reply arrives, paste it into the box labeled “paste their reply here.” Their row appearing in the node list is your confirmation, and the status flips to “connected.”

Joining. Open the link you were sent. It lands on a join card naming the world you've been invited to, with one button, “create my reply.” Nothing runs until you click it. The click builds your reply, copies it, and shows it in its own box. Send that back the same way the invite reached you, and keep the tab open. The connection completing is your confirmation. You never paste anything on this side.

Three rules keep this from getting confusing. A link invites one person, so mint a fresh one for the next. Both of you need to be at your machines at the same time. A link is a phone call, not a mailbox. Whoever joins can invite the next person, and once they do, everyone already connected joins in automatically, with nothing more to paste.

Nodes and names

A node is one browser holding a full copy of what's shared. Its name is two words drawn from the graph's own vocabulary, until you change it. Change yours in the “your node name” field in the sharing panel. Every other node you're connected to shows up in the node list beside it, most recently active first.

When something goes wrong

  • “this invite looks cut short”The copy lost part of the link. Ask for it again, and make sure the whole thing travels.
  • Pasting a reply did nothing visibleRead the message under the box. It says exactly what was wrong: cut short, an invite pasted where a reply belongs, or an invite that's already been used. Your text stays in the box so you can fix it.
  • “waiting for their reply” for a long timeNothing is wrong yet. Nothing happens until the other person sends their reply back and it's pasted. The wait ends when they do.
  • “that reply matched an invite that's already been used”Each link admits one person. Mint a fresh one with the invite button.
  • “your two machines couldn't find a path to each other”A public STUN server helps with most networks, but some firewalls or strict NATs still block it. Try again from a different network if the first attempt fails.
  • Someone's row dimsThey closed their tab or dropped offline. Everything they contributed stays, and a fresh invite reconnects them.
